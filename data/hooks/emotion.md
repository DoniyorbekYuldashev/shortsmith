# Emotion and identity

Hooks that speak to who the viewer is or how they feel. They are the most shareable kind, and
the easiest to overdo: keep them warm and honest, never guilt-tripping or divisive.

## Identity callout

**Template:** "If you grew up in [place], you know [sound / taste / moment]."

**Examples:**
- en: "If you grew up in Tashkent, you know this sound."
- uz: "Toshkentda o'sgan bo'lsangiz, bu ovozni taniysiz."
- ru: "Если вы выросли в Ташкенте, вы знаете этот звук."

**When to use:** local audiences; shared memories tied to a place or generation.

**Why it works:** recognition feels personal, and people share things that say who they are.

## Nostalgia

**Template:** "Remember when [thing] cost [old price]?"

**Examples:**
- en: "Remember when samsa cost 500 so'm?"
- uz: "Somsa 500 so'm bo'lgan paytlarni eslaysizmi?"
- ru: "Помните, когда самса стоила 500 сумов?"

**When to use:** prices, toys, TV, school life: anything that changed for an age group.

**Why it works:** nostalgia is a warm emotion, and comments fill up with people adding their own memories.

## Relatable struggle

**Template:** "When you [small, common frustration]..."

**Examples:**
- en: "When the plov is ready but the guests aren't."
- uz: "Osh tayyor, lekin mehmonlar hali yo'q."
- ru: "Когда плов готов, а гостей всё нет."

**When to use:** humour, everyday life, family situations.

**Why it works:** shared small frustrations make people feel understood, and they tag the friend it describes.

## Last chance

**Template:** "[Thing] ends on [date]. See it before then."

**Examples:**
- en: "The pomegranate season ends this month."
- uz: "Anor mavsumi shu oy tugaydi."
- ru: "Сезон гранатов заканчивается в этом месяце."

**When to use:** real deadlines: seasons, events, limited menus. Never a fake one.

**Why it works:** scarcity makes value feel higher, and a date gives a reason to act now.

## Try not to

**Template:** "Try not to [react] while watching this."

**Examples:**
- en: "Try not to get hungry watching this."
- uz: "Buni ko'rib qorningiz ochmasin-chi."
- ru: "Попробуйте не проголодаться."

**When to use:** food, cute animals, funny moments: content with a strong, predictable reaction.

**Why it works:** it turns watching into a small game the viewer usually loses, happily.

## Only some will get it

**Template:** "Only [group] will understand this."

**Examples:**
- en: "Only students in Tashkent will understand this."
- uz: "Buni faqat talabalar tushunadi."
- ru: "Это поймут только студенты."

**When to use:** inside jokes for a clear group (students, parents, drivers, coffee lovers).

**Why it works:** exclusivity makes the in-group feel seen and the out-group curious.

## Small kindness

**Template:** "[Someone] did [small kind act]. Here's what happened next."

**Examples:**
- en: "A stranger paid for my tea. So I did this."
- uz: "Notanish odam choyimga pul to'ladi. Men esa..."
- ru: "Незнакомец оплатил мой чай. И тогда я..."

**When to use:** community stories, hospitality, kindness between strangers.

**Why it works:** kindness is uplifting and highly shared, and "what happened next" keeps the story open.

**Watch out:** only with real events and with consent from the people shown.

## Hometown pride

**Template:** "Why [city] deserves more visitors."

**Examples:**
- en: "Why Khiva deserves more than a day trip."
- uz: "Nega Xivaga bir kundan ko'proq vaqt ajratish kerak."
- ru: "Почему Хива заслуживает больше одного дня."

**When to use:** underrated places, local businesses, cities with a strong identity.

**Why it works:** pride is energising; locals share it, and visitors get a reason to come.

## The friendly warning

**Template:** "Check [this] before you [buy / eat / go]."

**Examples:**
- en: "Check the bread's stamp before you buy it."
- uz: "Non olishdan oldin uning tamg'asiga qarang."
- ru: "Посмотрите на клеймо лепёшки перед покупкой."

**When to use:** consumer tips, safety, scams to avoid.

**Why it works:** protecting the viewer is caring, not preachy, and a warning is hard to scroll past.
