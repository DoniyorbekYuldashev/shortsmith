# Numbers, lists and stakes

Hooks built on a concrete number. Precise numbers ("47,000 so'm", "7 days") sound measured
and true; round ones ("lots", "many") sound like opinion.

## The precise number

**Template:** "I spent [exact amount] on [thing]. Was it worth it?"

**Examples:**
- en: "I spent 47,000 so'm on one breakfast. Worth it?"
- uz: "Bitta nonushtaga 47 ming so'm berdim. Arziydimi?"
- ru: "Отдал 47 тысяч сумов за завтрак. Стоило того?"

**When to use:** reviews, prices, experiments where you know the exact figure.

**Why it works:** an exact figure feels like evidence, and the question invites the viewer to judge for themselves.

## Three things

**Template:** "[Three] things to [do / order / see] at [place]."

**Examples:**
- en: "Three things to order at a Tashkent choyxona."
- uz: "Choyxonada albatta buyurtma qiling: uchta taom."
- ru: "Три блюда, которые надо заказать в чайхане."

**When to use:** recommendations with a short, fixed list; three fits a 30-second video.

**Why it works:** a number sets the length in advance, so viewers know the cost of staying and can track progress.

## Worst to best

**Template:** "Ranking every [X] in [place] from worst to best."

**Examples:**
- en: "Ranking Tashkent's samsa from worst to best."
- uz: "Toshkent somsalarini yomonidan zo'riga qarab baholadim."
- ru: "Рейтинг самсы Ташкента: от худшей к лучшей."

**When to use:** comparing several options of the same kind (dishes, viewpoints, cafés).

**Why it works:** a ranking builds toward number one, and people stay to see whether their favourite won (and comment if it didn't).

## Guess the price

**Template:** "Guess how much this [item] costs here."

**Examples:**
- en: "Guess how much this whole tray of fruit costs."
- uz: "Shu bir laganga qancha to'ladim, toping."
- ru: "Угадайте, сколько стоит этот поднос фруктов."

**When to use:** markets, travel and food content where the price surprises (very cheap or very expensive).

**Why it works:** everyone has a price intuition; the gap between the guess and the real number is the payoff.

## Thirty-second promise

**Template:** "Learn [skill] in the next 30 seconds."

**Examples:**
- en: "Learn to brew perfect green tea in 30 seconds."
- uz: "30 soniyada ko'k choyni to'g'ri damlashni o'rganing."
- ru: "Научитесь заваривать зелёный чай за 30 секунд."

**When to use:** one skill that really fits in a short video.

**Why it works:** the time cost is small and stated; the promise is concrete enough to judge at the end.

## One small change

**Template:** "One [small change] made my [thing] [much better]."

**Examples:**
- en: "One extra minute made my plov twice as good."
- uz: "Bir daqiqa ortiq damlash oshimni butunlay o'zgartirdi."
- ru: "Одна лишняя минута сделала плов вдвое вкуснее."

**When to use:** tips where a tiny cause has a visible effect.

**Why it works:** small effort with a big result is the most attractive trade there is, and it feels easy to copy.

## Cheap vs expensive

**Template:** "[Low price] vs [high price] [thing] in [city]."

**Examples:**
- en: "A 5,000 so'm coffee vs a 60,000 so'm coffee."
- uz: "5 minglik qahva va 60 minglik qahva: farqi bormi?"
- ru: "Кофе за 5 тысяч против кофе за 60 тысяч."

**When to use:** products and food with a wide price range in the same city.

**Why it works:** two numbers create a built-in question (is the expensive one worth it?) and a clear verdict to wait for.

## The extreme

**Template:** "The [oldest / biggest / cheapest] [thing] in [place]."

**Examples:**
- en: "The oldest working teahouse in Tashkent."
- uz: "Toshkentdagi eng qadimgi choyxona hali ham ishlaydi."
- ru: "Самая старая чайхана Ташкента до сих пор работает."

**When to use:** when the subject really holds a record or an extreme you can state honestly.

**Why it works:** superlatives promise something rare; seeing "the most" of anything is worth a few seconds.
