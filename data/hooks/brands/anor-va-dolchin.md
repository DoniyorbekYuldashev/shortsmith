# Anor va Dolchin (café, Tashkent)

A fictional brand, made up for this project to test brand-aware scripts. Any resemblance to a
real business is a coincidence.

## About and audience

Anor va Dolchin ("Pomegranate and Cinnamon") is a small café in Tashkent's Mirobod district:
twelve tables downstairs, a quiet room upstairs for people who work on laptops. The menu mixes
specialty coffee with Uzbek flavours: pomegranate cold brew, cinnamon and quince latte, green
tea brewed in the teapot, and non and pastries baked on site every morning. Regulars are
students and young professionals aged 20 to 35 who speak Uzbek and Russian, and some English.
They come for calm, good coffee and the feeling of being a guest, not a customer.

## Voice and tone

Warm, calm and a little playful, like a good host who remembers your order. Address viewers
politely ("siz" in Uzbek, "вы" in Russian) and speak in short, simple sentences. Celebrate
small moments: the first sip, steam on a cold morning, bread out of the oven. Confident, never
loud; we invite rather than sell. A touch of humour is welcome; sarcasm is not.

## Words we use and avoid

Use: shinam (cozy), mehmon (guest), mezbon (host), damlangan (brewed), uyda pishirilgan
(home-baked), mavsumiy (seasonal), "come by", "save the address", "your table is waiting".
Avoid: "the cheapest", "the best in the world", "luxury", "VIP", "hurry", "buy now", "limited
offer", discount talk of any kind, and comparisons with other cafés by name. Avoid English
buzzwords in Uzbek or Russian scripts ("vibe", "aesthetic", "content"). Never call the café a
restaurant or a bar.

## Visual style

Warm amber morning light from the window, terracotta walls, wooden tables, pomegranate-red
accents, steam rising from cups. Close-ups of hands pouring, breaking bread, stirring.
Natural light only; no neon, no harsh flash, no stock-photo smiles at the camera. Every image
prompt ends with the same style words: "warm amber light, terracotta and pomegranate red,
35mm film look, cozy".

## Content rules

Show the real café, real staff and real food; people only with their consent. Every video
mentions the season (autumn pomegranates, winter quince, spring green tea). Prices only when
they are current and exact. The call to action is an invitation ("come by this week", "save
the address"), never a hard sell. Keep it family-friendly: no alcohol, no politics, no jokes
about other businesses.
