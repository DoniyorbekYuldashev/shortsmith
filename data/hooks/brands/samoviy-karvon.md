# Samoviy Karvon (travel company, Uzbekistan)

A fictional brand, made up for this project to test brand-aware scripts. Any resemblance to a
real business is a coincidence.

## About and audience

Samoviy Karvon ("Heavenly Caravan") runs slow, small-group trips along the old Silk Road
cities: Samarkand, Bukhara, Khiva and the mountain villages in between. Groups are never
larger than eight people, guides are local historians and craftspeople, and nights are split
between family guesthouses and small hotels. Travellers are mostly 28 to 55, curious and
independent: English speakers from Europe and Asia, Russian-speaking travellers, and members
of the Uzbek diaspora showing their children where the family comes from.

## Voice and tone

Knowledgeable, unhurried and respectful. We tell stories, not lists of facts: who built this,
who lives here now, what the craftsman's grandfather taught him. Quietly adventurous: we
admire the place instead of shouting about it. Honest about practical things (heat in July,
long drives, simple guesthouse bathrooms). Speak to viewers as fellow travellers, never as
customers.

## Words we use and avoid

Use: slow travel, small group, local guide, guesthouse, craft, story, "walk with us", "the
road between cities", "join a small group". Avoid: "exotic", "undiscovered", "untouched",
"hidden gem", "off the beaten path", "bucket list", "cheap", "third world", "the Stans", and
any claim that we are "the only" or "the first". Avoid exaggerations like "breathtaking" and
"magical" in every line: show it instead.

## Visual style

Golden-hour light, wide landscapes with a person in frame for scale, then close details:
tilework, hands at a loom, bread stamps, spice piles. Earth tones with turquoise tile blue
as the accent colour. Natural colours, no heavy filters. Every image prompt ends with the same
style words: "golden hour light, earth tones and turquoise tile blue, documentary travel
photo".

## Content rules

Film respectfully at mosques, madrasas and homes: no filming during prayer, and local people
appear only with their consent. Never stage poverty or treat locals as scenery. Facts about
history must be checkable; when a story is a legend, say so. Show the group small (two to
eight people) because that is the promise. The call to action is "join a small group" or
"walk with us"; no countdown timers or fake scarcity.
